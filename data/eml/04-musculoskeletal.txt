# 4 | The Musculoskeletal System

## Bone structure and function
T skeletal system = the bones and joints that support the body, protect organs, store minerals and make blood cells
T osteocyte = a mature bone cell | oste/o + -cyte
T osteoblast = a bone cell that builds new bone | oste/o + -blast
T osteoclast = a bone cell that breaks down and reabsorbs bone | oste/o + -clast
T compact bone = the dense, hard outer layer of bone
T spongy bone = lightweight bone with a honeycomb structure, found inside bone ends; also called cancellous bone
T periosteum = the tough membrane covering the outer surface of a bone | peri- + oste/o + -um
T diaphysis = the shaft of a long bone
T epiphysis = the wider end of a long bone
T epiphyseal plate = the growth plate of cartilage where a long bone grows in length
T medullary cavity = the hollow center of a long bone's shaft that holds yellow marrow
T red bone marrow = soft tissue inside bones that produces blood cells; hematopoiesis
T yellow bone marrow = fatty marrow that stores fat
T hematopoiesis = the formation of blood cells | hemat/o + -poiesis
T ossification = the process of bone formation | oss/i + -fication
T cartilage = smooth, flexible connective tissue that covers bone ends and forms parts of the ear and nose
T long bone = a bone longer than it is wide, such as the femur
T short bone = a cube-shaped bone, such as the carpals of the wrist
T flat bone = a thin, flat bone, such as the sternum or skull bones
T irregular bone = a bone with a complex shape, such as a vertebra
T sesamoid bone = a small bone embedded in a tendon, such as the patella
P oste/o, oss/i = bone
P chondr/o = cartilage
P myel/o = bone marrow, spinal cord
P -blast = immature cell, germ cell
P -clast = to break
P -poiesis = formation

## Bones of the skull and axial skeleton
T axial skeleton = the skull, vertebral column, ribs and sternum
T appendicular skeleton = the bones of the limbs plus the shoulder and pelvic girdles
T cranium = the part of the skull that encloses the brain | crani/o + -um | @skeleton.cranium
T frontal bone = the skull bone forming the forehead | @skull.frontal
T parietal bone = one of a pair of skull bones forming the top and upper sides of the head | @skull.parietal
T temporal bone = one of a pair of skull bones at the sides of the head that contain the ear structures | @skull.temporal
T occipital bone = the skull bone forming the back and base of the head, with the opening for the spinal cord | @skull.occipital
T sphenoid bone = the bat-shaped bone at the base of the skull behind the eyes | @skull.sphenoid
T ethmoid bone = a light, spongy bone between the eyes forming part of the nasal cavity
T zygomatic bone = the cheekbone | @skull.zygomatic
T maxilla = the upper jawbone, which holds the upper teeth | @skull.maxilla
T mandible = the lower jawbone; the only movable bone of the skull | @skull.mandible
T nasal bone = one of a pair of small bones forming the bridge of the nose | @skull.nasal
T suture of the skull = an immovable joint where skull bones meet
T fontanelle = a soft spot between the skull bones of an infant
T foramen magnum = the large opening in the occipital bone where the spinal cord passes
T sinus = an air-filled cavity in a skull bone
T sternum = the breastbone, at the center of the chest | stern/o + -um | @skeleton.sternum
T xiphoid process = the small lower tip of the sternum
T ribs = the 12 pairs of curved bones that form the rib cage | @skeleton.ribs
T true ribs = the first seven pairs of ribs, which attach directly to the sternum
T floating ribs = the last two pairs of ribs, which do not attach to the sternum in front
T vertebral column = the spine; the stack of vertebrae that protects the spinal cord | @skeleton.vertebrae
T vertebra = one of the bones of the spinal column
T cervical vertebrae = the 7 vertebrae of the neck | cervic/o + -al | @spine.cervical
T thoracic vertebrae = the 12 vertebrae of the chest that connect to the ribs | thorac/o + -ic | @spine.thoracic
T lumbar vertebrae = the 5 large vertebrae of the lower back | lumb/o + -ar | @spine.lumbar
T sacrum = the triangular bone of 5 fused vertebrae at the base of the spine | @spine.sacrum
T coccyx = the tailbone, made of small fused vertebrae | @spine.coccyx
T intervertebral disk = a cartilage pad between vertebrae that cushions them | inter- + vertebr/o + -al
P crani/o = skull, cranium
P vertebr/o, spondyl/o = vertebra
P cervic/o = neck
P lumb/o = lower back
P stern/o = sternum
P cost/o = rib

## Bones of the limbs and girdles
T clavicle = the collarbone | @skeleton.clavicle
T scapula = the shoulder blade
T humerus = the bone of the upper arm | @skeleton.humerus
T radius = the forearm bone on the thumb side | @skeleton.radius
T ulna = the forearm bone on the little-finger side | @skeleton.ulna
T olecranon = the bony point of the elbow, part of the ulna
T carpals = the 8 small bones of the wrist | @skeleton.carpals
T metacarpals = the 5 bones of the palm of the hand | meta- + carp/o + -al | @skeleton.metacarpals
T phalanges = the bones of the fingers and toes | @skeleton.phalanges
T pelvis = the basin-shaped ring of bones formed by the hip bones and sacrum | @skeleton.pelvis
T ischium = the lower, back part of the hip bone that you sit on
T pubis = the front part of the hip bone
T acetabulum = the cup-shaped socket of the hip bone that holds the head of the femur
T femur = the thighbone; the longest and strongest bone in the body | @skeleton.femur
T patella = the kneecap | @skeleton.patella
T tibia = the larger, inner bone of the lower leg; the shinbone | @skeleton.tibia
T fibula = the thinner, outer bone of the lower leg | @skeleton.fibula
T tarsals = the 7 bones of the ankle and back of the foot | @skeleton.tarsals
T calcaneus = the heel bone
T metatarsals = the 5 bones of the middle of the foot | meta- + tars/o + -al | @skeleton.metatarsals
T malleolus = a bony bump on either side of the ankle
P carp/o = wrist
P tars/o = ankle
P phalang/o = finger or toe bone
P femor/o = femur
P patell/o = kneecap
P pelv/i = pelvis, hip bone

## Joints and connective structures
T joint = a place where two or more bones meet; also called an articulation
T synovial joint = a freely movable joint with a fluid-filled cavity, such as the knee
T joint capsule = the fibrous sac that surrounds and encloses a synovial joint | @joint.capsule
T synovial membrane = the lining of a joint capsule that produces synovial fluid | @joint.membrane
T synovial fluid = the slippery fluid that lubricates a joint | @joint.fluid
T articular cartilage = the smooth cartilage that covers bone ends inside a joint | @joint.cartilage
T ligament = a tough band of fibrous tissue that connects bone to bone | @joint.ligament
T tendon = a tough cord of fibrous tissue that connects muscle to bone | @joint.tendon
T bursa = a small fluid-filled sac that reduces friction near a joint
T meniscus = a crescent-shaped pad of cartilage in the knee
T anterior cruciate ligament = a key ligament inside the knee that keeps the tibia from sliding forward
T rotator cuff = the group of four muscles and their tendons that stabilize the shoulder
T ball-and-socket joint = a joint allowing movement in many directions, such as the hip and shoulder
T hinge joint = a joint that moves in one plane like a door, such as the elbow and knee
P arthr/o, articul/o = joint
P ligament/o = ligament
P ten/o, tend/o, tendin/o = tendon
P burs/o = bursa
P synov/o = synovial membrane, synovial fluid
P menisc/o = meniscus

## Muscles and body movements
T skeletal muscle = voluntary striated muscle attached to bones that moves the body | @joint.muscle
T smooth muscle = involuntary muscle in the walls of organs and blood vessels
T cardiac muscle = involuntary striated muscle found only in the heart
T fascia = a sheet of fibrous tissue that surrounds and separates muscles
T origin = the attachment of a muscle to the bone that stays still
T insertion = the attachment of a muscle to the bone that moves
T flexion = bending a joint to decrease the angle between bones
T extension = straightening a joint to increase the angle between bones
T abduction = moving a limb away from the midline of the body | ab- + duct/o + -ion
T adduction = moving a limb toward the midline of the body | ad- + duct/o + -ion
T rotation = turning a bone around its own axis
T circumduction = moving a limb in a circle
T pronation = turning the palm downward or backward
T supination = turning the palm upward or forward
T dorsiflexion = bending the foot upward toward the shin | dors/o + flexion
T plantar flexion = pointing the foot downward
T range of motion = how far a joint can move in each direction
T deltoid = the triangular muscle covering the shoulder, a common injection site
T quadriceps = the group of four muscles on the front of the thigh that extend the knee
T hamstrings = the muscles at the back of the thigh that bend the knee
T biceps = the muscle on the front of the upper arm that bends the elbow
T triceps = the muscle on the back of the upper arm that straightens the elbow
P my/o, muscul/o = muscle
P fasci/o = fascia
P -asthenia = weakness
P kinesi/o = movement

## Fractures and bone disorders
T fracture = a break in a bone
T closed fracture = a broken bone that does not break through the skin; also called simple fracture
T open fracture = a broken bone that breaks through the skin; also called compound fracture
T comminuted fracture = a bone broken or crushed into several pieces
T greenstick fracture = an incomplete break in which the bone bends and cracks on one side, common in children
T compression fracture = a bone collapsed by pressure, often a vertebra
T spiral fracture = a break that twists around the bone, from a twisting force
T stress fracture = a tiny crack in a bone from repeated stress
T pathologic fracture = a break in bone weakened by disease, such as osteoporosis or cancer
T displaced fracture = a break in which the bone ends have moved out of alignment
T Colles fracture = a break of the radius just above the wrist, often from a fall on an outstretched hand
T reduction = putting broken bone ends back in their proper position
T callus = the new bone tissue that forms around a fracture as it heals
T osteoporosis = loss of bone density making bones porous and fragile | oste/o + por/o + -osis
T osteopenia = lower than normal bone density, not yet osteoporosis | oste/o + -penia
T osteomyelitis = infection and inflammation of bone and bone marrow | oste/o + myel/o + -itis
T osteomalacia = softening of bones from lack of vitamin D or calcium | oste/o + -malacia
T rickets = softening and bending of bones in children from lack of vitamin D
T osteosarcoma = a malignant bone tumor | oste/o + sarc/o + -oma
T osteogenesis imperfecta = an inherited disorder causing brittle bones that break easily
T Paget disease = a disorder of disorganized bone remodeling causing enlarged, weak bones
P por/o = pore, small opening

## Joint and spine disorders
T arthritis = inflammation of a joint | arthr/o + -itis
T osteoarthritis = degenerative joint disease with wearing away of cartilage | oste/o + arthr/o + -itis
T rheumatoid arthritis = a chronic autoimmune disease that inflames the synovial lining of many joints
T gout = joint inflammation caused by uric acid crystals, often in the big toe
T ankylosing spondylitis = inflammation of the spine leading to fused, stiff vertebrae | spondyl/o + -itis
T arthralgia = joint pain | arthr/o + -algia
T bursitis = inflammation of a bursa | burs/o + -itis
T sprain = a stretched or torn ligament
T strain = a stretched or torn muscle or tendon
T dislocation = displacement of a bone out of its joint
T subluxation = a partial dislocation of a joint
T tendinitis = inflammation of a tendon | tendin/o + -itis
T carpal tunnel syndrome = numbness and pain in the hand from pressure on the median nerve at the wrist
T kyphosis = an exaggerated outward curve of the upper back; hunchback
T lordosis = an exaggerated inward curve of the lower back; swayback
T scoliosis = an abnormal sideways curve of the spine
T herniated disk = a disk whose soft center pushes through its outer ring and may press on a nerve
T spinal stenosis = narrowing of the spinal canal that can press on nerves
T sciatica = pain along the sciatic nerve down the back of the leg
T myalgia = muscle pain | my/o + -algia
T fibromyalgia = a chronic condition of widespread muscle pain, tenderness and fatigue | fibr/o + my/o + -algia
T muscular dystrophy = an inherited group of diseases causing progressive muscle weakness | dys- + -trophy
T myasthenia gravis = an autoimmune disease causing muscle weakness and fatigue | my/o + -asthenia
T rhabdomyolysis = breakdown of skeletal muscle that releases harmful proteins into the blood | rhabd/o + my/o + -lysis
T atrophy of muscle = wasting away of muscle from disuse or disease
P rheumat/o = watery flow; joints
P kyph/o = humpback
P lord/o = swayback, curve
P scoli/o = crooked, curved
P fibr/o = fiber

## Diagnosis and treatment
T arthroscopy = looking inside a joint with a small camera | arthr/o + -scopy
T arthrocentesis = puncturing a joint to remove fluid | arthr/o + -centesis
T arthroplasty = surgical repair or replacement of a joint | arthr/o + -plasty
T arthrodesis = surgical fusion of a joint | arthr/o + -desis
T total knee arthroplasty = replacement of the knee joint with an artificial joint
T total hip arthroplasty = replacement of the hip joint with an artificial joint
T bone density scan = a low-dose x-ray test that measures bone strength to detect osteoporosis
T electromyography = recording the electrical activity of muscles | electr/o + my/o + -graphy
T laminectomy = removal of part of a vertebra (the lamina) to relieve pressure on nerves | lamin/o + -ectomy
T spinal fusion = joining two or more vertebrae so they heal into one solid bone
T open reduction internal fixation = surgery that realigns a broken bone and holds it with plates, screws or rods
T closed reduction = realigning a broken bone without cutting the skin
T traction = a pulling force used to align bones or relieve pressure
T cast = a rigid wrap that holds a broken bone still while it heals
T orthosis = a brace or splint that supports a body part | orth/o + -osis
T prosthesis = an artificial body part, such as a leg or joint
T amputation = surgical removal of a limb or part of a limb
T physical therapy = treatment using exercise and movement to restore function
T NSAIDs = drugs such as ibuprofen that reduce pain and inflammation
T muscle relaxant = a drug that relieves muscle spasms
T bisphosphonate = a drug that slows bone loss, used for osteoporosis
P -desis = binding, fusion
P lamin/o = lamina (part of a vertebra)
P orth/o = straight
P electr/o = electricity
