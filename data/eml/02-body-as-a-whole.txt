# 2 | The Body as a Whole

## Levels of organization
T cell = the smallest structural and functional unit of life
T tissue = a group of similar cells working together to perform a function
T organ = a structure made of two or more tissue types that performs a specific job
T organ system = a group of organs that work together, such as the digestive system
T organism = a complete living being made of interacting organ systems
T homeostasis = the body's ability to keep a stable internal environment | home/o + -stasis
T metabolism = all the chemical processes that build up and break down substances in the body
T anabolism = the building of complex molecules from simpler ones
T catabolism = the breakdown of complex molecules into simpler ones, releasing energy

## Cells and genetics
T cell membrane = the outer boundary of a cell that controls what enters and leaves | @cell.membrane
T cytoplasm = the gel-like material inside a cell surrounding the nucleus | cyt/o + -plasm | @cell.cytoplasm
T nucleus = the control center of the cell that contains the DNA | @cell.nucleus
T mitochondria = organelles that produce energy (ATP) for the cell | @cell.mitochondria
T ribosome = an organelle where proteins are made | @cell.ribosome
T nucleolus = a dense region inside the nucleus that makes ribosomes | @cell.nucleolus
T endoplasmic reticulum = a network of membranes that makes and transports proteins and lipids | @cell.er
T Golgi apparatus = an organelle that packages and ships proteins out of the cell | @cell.golgi
T chromosome = a structure in the nucleus made of DNA that carries genes; humans have 46
T gene = a segment of DNA that carries the code for a specific trait or protein
T DNA = the molecule that stores genetic information
T genome = the complete set of genetic material in an organism
T mitosis = cell division that produces two identical cells
T meiosis = cell division that produces sex cells with half the chromosome number
T mutation = a permanent change in the DNA sequence
T congenital = present at birth
T hereditary = passed from parents to children through genes
T stem cell = an unspecialized cell that can develop into different cell types
T karyotype = a picture of a person's chromosomes arranged in pairs | kary/o + -type
P cyt/o = cell
P kary/o, nucle/o = nucleus
P gen/o = producing, origin, gene
P hist/o = tissue

## Tissues
T epithelial tissue = tissue that covers body surfaces and lines cavities and organs | epi- + thel/i + -al
T connective tissue = tissue that supports and binds other tissues, such as bone, blood and fat
T muscle tissue = tissue that contracts to produce movement
T nervous tissue = tissue that sends and receives electrical impulses
T histology = the study of tissues | hist/o + -logy
T hyperplasia = an increase in the number of cells in a tissue | hyper- + -plasia
T hypertrophy = an increase in the size of cells or an organ | hyper- + -trophy
T atrophy = wasting away or decrease in size of a tissue or organ | a- + -trophy
T dysplasia = abnormal development or growth of cells | dys- + -plasia
T neoplasm = a new, abnormal growth of tissue; a tumor | neo- + -plasm
T benign = not cancerous and not spreading
T malignant = cancerous, able to invade and spread
P -plasia = formation, development
P -plasm = formed substance

## Anatomical position and directions
T anatomical position = standing upright, facing forward, arms at the sides with palms forward
T superior = above or toward the head
T inferior = below or toward the feet
T anterior = toward the front of the body
T posterior = toward the back of the body
T ventral = on the belly side; same as anterior in humans
T dorsal = on the back side; same as posterior in humans
T medial = toward the midline of the body
T lateral = away from the midline, toward the side
T proximal = closer to the point of attachment or the trunk
T distal = farther from the point of attachment or the trunk
T superficial = toward or at the body surface
T deep = away from the body surface
T cephalic = toward the head | cephal/o + -ic
T caudal = toward the tail or lower body
T supine = lying on the back, face up
T prone = lying on the abdomen, face down
T bilateral = on both sides | bi- + later/o + -al
T unilateral = on one side only | uni- + later/o + -al
P anter/o = front
P poster/o = back, behind
P medi/o = middle
P later/o = side
P proxim/o = near
P dist/o = far, away from
P cephal/o = head
P ventr/o = belly side
P dors/o = back

## Planes and body cavities
T sagittal plane = a vertical plane dividing the body into right and left parts | @planes.sagittal
T midsagittal plane = the sagittal plane exactly at the midline, creating equal right and left halves
T frontal plane = a vertical plane dividing the body into front and back parts; also called coronal | @planes.frontal
T transverse plane = a horizontal plane dividing the body into upper and lower parts | @planes.transverse
T cranial cavity = the space inside the skull that holds the brain | @cavities.cranial
T spinal cavity = the space inside the vertebral column that holds the spinal cord | @cavities.spinal
T thoracic cavity = the chest cavity, holding the heart and lungs | @cavities.thoracic
T abdominal cavity = the cavity holding the stomach, intestines, liver and other digestive organs | @cavities.abdominal
T pelvic cavity = the cavity holding the urinary bladder and reproductive organs | @cavities.pelvic
T dorsal cavity = the back body cavity, made of the cranial and spinal cavities | @cavities.dorsal
T ventral cavity = the front body cavity, made of the thoracic, abdominal and pelvic cavities | @cavities.ventral
T diaphragm = the dome-shaped muscle separating the thoracic and abdominal cavities | @cavities.diaphragm
T mediastinum = the central area of the chest between the lungs, holding the heart
T peritoneum = the membrane that lines the abdominal cavity and covers its organs | periton/o + -eum
T pleura = the membrane that surrounds the lungs and lines the chest cavity
T viscera = the internal organs, especially those in the abdomen
P thorac/o = chest
P abdomin/o = abdomen
P pelv/i = pelvis
P viscer/o = internal organs

## Abdominal quadrants and regions
T right upper quadrant = the abdominal area holding most of the liver and the gallbladder | @quadrants.ruq
T left upper quadrant = the abdominal area holding the stomach and spleen | @quadrants.luq
T right lower quadrant = the abdominal area holding the appendix | @quadrants.rlq
T left lower quadrant = the abdominal area holding the sigmoid colon | @quadrants.llq
T epigastric region = the upper middle abdominal region, above the stomach | epi- + gastr/o + -ic | @regions.epigastric
T umbilical region = the central abdominal region around the navel | @regions.umbilical
T hypogastric region = the lower middle abdominal region below the navel; also called pubic | hypo- + gastr/o + -ic | @regions.hypogastric
T right hypochondriac region = the upper right abdominal region, under the ribs | hypo- + chondr/o + -iac | @regions.rhypo
T left hypochondriac region = the upper left abdominal region, under the ribs | @regions.lhypo
T right lumbar region = the right side region of the abdomen at waist level | @regions.rlumbar
T left lumbar region = the left side region of the abdomen at waist level | @regions.llumbar
T right iliac region = the lower right abdominal region near the groin; also called inguinal | @regions.riliac
T left iliac region = the lower left abdominal region near the groin; also called inguinal | @regions.liliac
T umbilicus = the navel, where the umbilical cord was attached
T inguinal = relating to the groin

## Body systems and disease
T integumentary system = the skin, hair, nails and skin glands; protects the body
T musculoskeletal system = the bones, joints and muscles; supports and moves the body
T cardiovascular system = the heart and blood vessels; moves blood through the body
T respiratory system = the airways and lungs; exchanges oxygen and carbon dioxide
T digestive system = the organs that break down food and absorb nutrients
T urinary system = the kidneys, ureters, bladder and urethra; filters blood and removes wastes
T nervous system = the brain, spinal cord and nerves; controls and coordinates the body
T endocrine system = glands that release hormones into the blood
T lymphatic system = vessels and organs that return fluid to the blood and help fight infection
T inflammation = the body's protective response to injury or infection, with redness, heat, swelling and pain
T infection = invasion of the body by disease-causing organisms
T pathogen = a microorganism that causes disease | path/o + -gen
T edema = swelling caused by excess fluid in the tissues
T necrosis = death of cells or tissue | necr/o + -osis
P path/o = disease
P necr/o = death
