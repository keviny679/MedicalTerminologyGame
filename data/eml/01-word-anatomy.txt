# 1 | The Anatomy of Medical Terms

## Building medical words
T root = the core part of a medical word that carries its main meaning
T combining vowel = a vowel (usually o) added after a root to link it to another word part
T combining form = a root plus its combining vowel, written with a slash, such as cardi/o
T prefix = a word part placed before a root to change its meaning
T suffix = a word part placed at the end of a word to change or complete its meaning
T word analysis = breaking a medical term into its parts to work out its meaning
T definition by parts = defining a term by starting with the suffix, then the prefix, then the root
T eponym = a disease, structure or procedure named after a person, such as Alzheimer disease
T acronym = an abbreviation formed from first letters and pronounced as a word, such as laser
T abbreviation = a shortened form of a word or phrase, such as BP for blood pressure
T homonym = a word that sounds like another word but has a different meaning, such as ileum and ilium
T diagnosis = identifying a disease from its signs, symptoms and tests | dia- + gnos/o + -sis
T prognosis = the predicted outcome or course of a disease | pro- + gnos/o + -sis
T sign = objective evidence of disease that can be observed or measured, such as a fever
T symptom = a subjective sensation of disease that the patient reports, such as pain
T syndrome = a set of signs and symptoms that occur together and point to a particular condition
T acute = describes a condition with sudden onset and short duration
T chronic = describes a condition that persists for a long time or keeps coming back
T etiology = the study or the cause of a disease | eti/o + -logy
T idiopathic = describes a disease of unknown cause | idi/o + path/o + -ic
T iatrogenic = describes a problem caused by medical treatment | iatr/o + gen/o + -ic
T nosocomial = describes an infection acquired in a hospital or health care setting
T pathology = the study of disease | path/o + -logy
T physiology = the study of how the body and its parts function | physi/o + -logy
T anatomy = the study of the structure of the body

## Plurals and pronunciation
P -a to -ae = singular ending that becomes -ae in the plural, as vertebra to vertebrae
P -is to -es = singular ending that becomes -es in the plural, as diagnosis to diagnoses
P -um to -a = singular ending that becomes -a in the plural, as bacterium to bacteria
P -us to -i = singular ending that becomes -i in the plural, as bronchus to bronchi
P -ix, -ex to -ices = singular endings that become -ices in the plural, as appendix to appendices
P -on to -a = singular ending that becomes -a in the plural, as ganglion to ganglia
P -ma to -mata = singular ending that becomes -mata in the plural, as stoma to stomata
P -nx to -nges = singular ending that becomes -nges in the plural, as phalanx to phalanges
T ilium = the large upper wing-shaped bone of the pelvis; sounds like ileum
T ileum = the last section of the small intestine; sounds like ilium

## The health care team
T physician = a licensed medical doctor (MD or DO) who diagnoses and treats disease
T physician assistant = a licensed clinician who practices medicine as part of a physician-led team
T nurse practitioner = an advanced practice registered nurse who can diagnose, treat and prescribe
T registered nurse = a licensed nurse who plans and delivers patient care
T medical assistant = a team member who performs clinical and administrative tasks in a medical office
T pharmacist = a licensed professional who prepares and dispenses medications and advises on their use
T pediatrician = a physician specializing in the care of children | ped/o + iatr/o + -ician
T geriatrician = a physician specializing in the care of older adults | ger/o + iatr/o + -ician
T dermatologist = a specialist in diseases of the skin | dermat/o + -logist
T cardiologist = a specialist in diseases of the heart | cardi/o + -logist
T orthopedist = a specialist in disorders of bones, joints and muscles | orth/o + ped/o + -ist
T electronic health record = a digital record of a patient's health information shared by providers
T HIPAA = the US law that protects the privacy and security of patient health information
