# 6 | The Cardiovascular System

## Structure of the heart
> The [[heart]] is a muscular pump about the size of a fist, sitting in the chest between the lungs with its
> pointed [[apex|apex of the heart]] aimed down and to the left. It has four chambers. The two upper chambers
> receive blood: the [[right atrium]] and [[left atrium]]. The two lower chambers pump blood out: the [[right
> ventricle]] and [[left ventricle]]. The left ventricle has the thickest wall because it pumps blood to the
> whole body. A wall called the [[interventricular septum]] separates the two ventricles. The heart wall has
> three layers. The inner lining is the [[endocardium]], the thick muscle layer is the [[myocardium]], and the
> outer layer is the [[epicardium]]. Around the heart is a protective sac, the [[pericardium]]. The heart muscle
> gets its own blood supply from the [[coronary arteries]], which branch off the aorta and wrap around the heart
> like a crown. Combining forms include [[cardi/o]] for heart, [[atri/o]] for atrium and [[ventricul/o]] for
> ventricle.
T heart = the hollow, muscular organ that pumps blood through the body
T right atrium = the upper right chamber of the heart; receives oxygen-poor blood from the body | @heart.ra
T right ventricle = the lower right chamber; pumps blood to the lungs | @heart.rv
T left atrium = the upper left chamber; receives oxygen-rich blood from the lungs | @heart.la
T left ventricle = the lower left chamber with the thickest wall; pumps blood to the whole body | @heart.lv
T interventricular septum = the muscular wall dividing the right and left ventricles | inter- + ventricul/o + -ar | @heart.septum
T pericardium = the double-layered sac that surrounds the heart | peri- + cardi/o + -um | @heart.pericardium
T epicardium = the outer layer of the heart wall | epi- + cardi/o + -um
T myocardium = the thick middle muscle layer of the heart wall | my/o + cardi/o + -um | @heart.myocardium
T endocardium = the thin inner lining of the heart chambers and valves | endo- + cardi/o + -um
T apex of the heart = the pointed lower tip of the heart, formed by the left ventricle
T coronary arteries = the arteries that supply the heart muscle itself with blood
P cardi/o = heart
P atri/o = atrium
P ventricul/o = ventricle
P coron/o = crown; coronary arteries
P pericardi/o = pericardium

## Heart valves and blood flow
> Blood flows through the heart in one direction, kept on track by four valves. Oxygen-poor blood returns from
> the body through the [[superior vena cava]] and [[inferior vena cava]] into the right atrium. It passes
> through the [[tricuspid valve]] into the right ventricle, then through the [[pulmonary valve]] into the
> [[pulmonary trunk]], which splits into the [[pulmonary arteries]] going to the lungs. These are the only
> arteries that carry oxygen-poor blood. In the lungs, blood picks up oxygen and returns through the [[pulmonary
> veins]] to the left atrium. This loop between heart and lungs is the [[pulmonary circulation]]. Blood then
> passes through the [[mitral valve]] into the left ventricle, then through the [[aortic valve]] into the
> [[aorta]], the body's largest artery, which sends it everywhere else; this loop is the [[systemic
> circulation]]. The valve flaps between atria and ventricles are anchored by cord-like [[chordae tendineae]],
> which stop them from flipping backward. Closing valves make the lub-dub sounds of the heartbeat.
T heart valve = a flap of tissue that keeps blood flowing in one direction through the heart
T tricuspid valve = the valve between the right atrium and right ventricle | @heart.tricuspid
T pulmonary valve = the valve between the right ventricle and the pulmonary trunk | @heart.pulmValve
T mitral valve = the valve between the left atrium and left ventricle; also called bicuspid valve | @heart.mitral
T aortic valve = the valve between the left ventricle and the aorta | @heart.aorticValve
T superior vena cava = the large vein bringing blood from the upper body to the right atrium | @heart.svc
T inferior vena cava = the large vein bringing blood from the lower body to the right atrium | @heart.ivc
T aorta = the largest artery, carrying oxygen-rich blood from the left ventricle to the body | @heart.aorta
T pulmonary trunk = the vessel carrying oxygen-poor blood from the right ventricle toward the lungs | @heart.pulmonary
T pulmonary arteries = the vessels carrying oxygen-poor blood from the pulmonary trunk to each lung | @heart.pulmArteries
T pulmonary veins = the vessels returning oxygen-rich blood from the lungs to the left atrium | @heart.pulmVeins
T pulmonary circulation = the flow of blood from the heart to the lungs and back
T systemic circulation = the flow of blood from the heart to the body and back
T chordae tendineae = string-like cords that hold the valve flaps to the ventricle walls
P valv/o, valvul/o = valve
P aort/o = aorta
P pulmon/o = lung

## Conduction system and heart rhythm
> The heart has its own electrical system. Each beat starts in the [[sinoatrial node]], the natural pacemaker in
> the wall of the right atrium. The signal spreads across both atria, making them contract, and reaches the
> [[atrioventricular node]], which pauses it briefly so the ventricles can fill. The signal then travels down
> the [[bundle of His]], splits into the right and left [[bundle branches]], and spreads through the [[Purkinje
> fibers]] in the ventricle walls, making the ventricles contract. One full heartbeat is a [[cardiac cycle]]:
> the contraction phase is [[systole]], and the relaxation and filling phase is [[diastole]]. A normal rhythm
> started by the SA node is [[sinus rhythm]]. A heart rate below 60 beats per minute is [[bradycardia]], and
> above 100 is [[tachycardia]]. Any irregular rhythm is an [[arrhythmia]]. In [[atrial fibrillation]] the atria
> quiver chaotically, raising the risk of clots. [[ventricular fibrillation]] is a deadly quivering of the
> ventricles that causes [[cardiac arrest]] unless the heart is shocked.
T sinoatrial node = the heart's natural pacemaker in the right atrium that starts each heartbeat | @conduction.sa
T atrioventricular node = the node that relays the electrical signal from the atria to the ventricles | @conduction.av
T bundle of His = the pathway that carries the signal from the AV node into the septum | @conduction.his
T bundle branches = right and left pathways in the septum that carry the signal to each ventricle | @conduction.branches
T Purkinje fibers = fibers that spread the signal through the ventricle walls so they contract | @conduction.purkinje
T systole = the contraction phase of the heartbeat
T diastole = the relaxation phase of the heartbeat, when the chambers fill
T cardiac cycle = one complete heartbeat, from one contraction to the next
T sinus rhythm = the normal heart rhythm started by the SA node
T pulse = the throb of an artery felt with each heartbeat
T cardiac output = the amount of blood the heart pumps per minute
T bradycardia = an abnormally slow heart rate, under 60 beats per minute | brady- + cardi/o + -ia
T tachycardia = an abnormally fast heart rate, over 100 beats per minute | tachy- + cardi/o + -ia
T arrhythmia = any abnormal heart rhythm; also called dysrhythmia | a- + rhythm + -ia
T atrial fibrillation = a fast, irregular, quivering rhythm of the atria that raises stroke risk
T ventricular fibrillation = a life-threatening, chaotic quivering of the ventricles that stops pumping
T heart block = a delay or blockage of the electrical signal through the AV node
T palpitations = an awareness of a pounding, fluttering or racing heartbeat
T cardiac arrest = sudden loss of heart function, breathing and consciousness

## Blood vessels and blood pressure
> Blood travels away from the heart in each [[artery]], which branches into smaller vessels, each an
> [[arteriole]]. The arterioles feed the [[capillary]] networks, tiny thin-walled vessels where oxygen and
> nutrients pass into the tissues. Blood then drains into each [[venule]], which joins larger veins returning
> blood to the heart. A [[vein]] has thinner walls than an artery and contains valves to keep blood moving
> toward the heart. The open space inside a vessel is the [[lumen]]. Narrowing of vessels is
> [[vasoconstriction]], and widening is [[vasodilation]]. [[blood pressure]] is the force of blood against
> artery walls, written as two numbers. The top number, the [[systolic pressure]], is measured as the heart
> contracts; the bottom number, the [[diastolic pressure]], is measured as it relaxes. It is measured with a
> [[sphygmomanometer]] and a [[stethoscope]]; listening with a stethoscope is [[auscultation]]. Persistently
> high blood pressure is [[hypertension]], and low is [[hypotension]]. A drop in pressure on standing up,
> causing dizziness, is [[orthostatic hypotension]].
T artery = a vessel that carries blood away from the heart | arteri/o + -y | @vessels.artery
T arteriole = a small branch of an artery | arteri/o + -ole
T capillary = a tiny vessel where oxygen, nutrients and wastes are exchanged with tissues | @vessels.capillary
T venule = a small vein that collects blood from capillaries | ven/o + -ule
T vein = a vessel that carries blood back toward the heart | @vessels.vein
T lumen = the open space inside a blood vessel
T vasoconstriction = narrowing of a blood vessel | vas/o + constriction
T vasodilation = widening of a blood vessel | vas/o + dilation
T blood pressure = the force of blood against artery walls
T systolic pressure = the top number of blood pressure; pressure when the ventricles contract
T diastolic pressure = the bottom number of blood pressure; pressure when the heart relaxes
T sphygmomanometer = the instrument used to measure blood pressure | sphygm/o + man/o + -meter
T stethoscope = an instrument used to listen to heart, lung and bowel sounds | steth/o + -scope
T auscultation = listening to sounds within the body, usually with a stethoscope
T hypertension = persistently high blood pressure | hyper- + tens/o + -ion
T hypotension = abnormally low blood pressure | hypo- + tens/o + -ion
T orthostatic hypotension = a drop in blood pressure when standing up, causing dizziness
P angi/o, vas/o, vascul/o = vessel
P arteri/o = artery
P ven/o, phleb/o = vein
P sphygm/o = pulse
P steth/o = chest
P -meter = instrument for measuring
P thromb/o = clot
P ather/o = fatty plaque
P embol/o = plug

## Heart disorders
> The most common heart disease is [[coronary artery disease]]. It is usually caused by [[atherosclerosis]], the
> buildup of fatty plaque inside the arteries; hardening of the arteries in general is [[arteriosclerosis]].
> When narrowed arteries cannot deliver enough blood, the tissue suffers [[ischemia]], and the patient may feel
> chest pain called [[angina pectoris]]. If an artery is completely blocked, heart muscle dies; this is a
> [[myocardial infarction]], or heart attack, and the area of dead tissue is an [[infarct]]. When the heart
> cannot pump enough blood to meet the body's needs, the patient has [[heart failure]]; when fluid backs up into
> the lungs and legs it is called [[congestive heart failure]]. Disease of the heart muscle itself is
> [[cardiomyopathy]], and an enlarged heart is [[cardiomegaly]]. Inflammation is named by layer:
> [[endocarditis]], [[myocarditis]] and [[pericarditis]]. An abnormal heart sound from turbulent blood flow is a
> [[heart murmur]]. A valve that is too narrow has [[valvular stenosis]], while a leaky valve has [[valvular
> regurgitation]]. Fluid squeezing the heart inside the pericardium is [[cardiac tamponade]].
T coronary artery disease = narrowing of the coronary arteries by plaque, reducing blood flow to the heart
T atherosclerosis = buildup of fatty plaque that hardens and narrows arteries | ather/o + -sclerosis
T arteriosclerosis = hardening and thickening of artery walls | arteri/o + -sclerosis
T angina pectoris = chest pain caused by too little blood flow to the heart muscle
T myocardial infarction = a heart attack; death of heart muscle from blocked blood flow | my/o + cardi/o + -al
T ischemia = reduced blood supply to a tissue | isch/o + -emia
T infarct = an area of dead tissue caused by lack of blood supply
T heart failure = the heart cannot pump enough blood to meet the body's needs
T congestive heart failure = heart failure with fluid backing up into the lungs and body
T cardiomyopathy = disease of the heart muscle | cardi/o + my/o + -pathy
T cardiomegaly = enlargement of the heart | cardi/o + -megaly
T endocarditis = inflammation of the inner lining of the heart, usually from infection | endo- + cardi/o + -itis
T pericarditis = inflammation of the sac around the heart | pericardi/o + -itis
T myocarditis = inflammation of the heart muscle | my/o + cardi/o + -itis
T heart murmur = an abnormal heart sound, often from turbulent blood flow through a valve
T mitral valve prolapse = a mitral valve that bulges back into the left atrium during contraction
T valvular stenosis = narrowing of a heart valve that blocks blood flow
T valvular regurgitation = backward leaking of blood through a valve that does not close well
T congenital heart defect = a heart problem present at birth, such as a hole in the septum
T rheumatic heart disease = heart valve damage caused by rheumatic fever after strep infection
T cardiac tamponade = compression of the heart by fluid in the pericardial sac

## Vascular disorders
> An [[aneurysm]] is a bulging, weakened spot in an artery wall that can burst. A [[thrombus]] is a blood clot
> that forms in a vessel and stays there, while an [[embolus]] is a clot or other mass that breaks loose and
> travels; when it blocks a vessel, the blockage is an [[embolism]]. A clot in a deep leg vein is [[deep vein
> thrombosis]], which causes swelling and pain. If part of it breaks off and lodges in the lungs, it becomes a
> [[pulmonary embolism]], a medical emergency. Inflammation of a vein is [[phlebitis]], and with a clot it is
> [[thrombophlebitis]]. Twisted, swollen veins in the legs with weak valves are [[varicose veins]]. Narrowed
> arteries in the legs cause [[peripheral artery disease]], which produces [[claudication]], cramping leg pain
> when walking that eases with rest. In [[Raynaud phenomenon]], fingers turn white and blue in the cold as small
> arteries spasm. High fat levels in the blood are [[hyperlipidemia]]. Severe bleeding is a [[hemorrhage]], and
> failure of circulation to deliver enough blood to organs is [[shock]]. A [[stroke]] occurs when blood flow to
> the brain is cut off.
T aneurysm = a balloon-like bulge in the wall of an artery
T thrombus = a blood clot that forms and stays in a vessel | thromb/o + -us
T embolus = a clot or other mass that travels through the bloodstream | embol/o + -us
T embolism = blockage of a vessel by an embolus | embol/o + -ism
T deep vein thrombosis = a clot in a deep vein, usually in the leg | thromb/o + -osis
T pulmonary embolism = a clot that travels to and blocks an artery in the lungs
T thrombophlebitis = inflammation of a vein with a clot | thromb/o + phleb/o + -itis
T phlebitis = inflammation of a vein | phleb/o + -itis
T varicose veins = swollen, twisted veins, usually in the legs, from weak valves
T peripheral artery disease = narrowing of arteries to the limbs, causing leg pain when walking
T claudication = leg pain or cramping with walking from poor blood flow
T Raynaud phenomenon = spasms of small arteries in the fingers and toes that turn them white or blue in the cold
T hyperlipidemia = high levels of fats such as cholesterol in the blood | hyper- + lip/o + -emia
T shock = a dangerous drop in blood flow to organs
T hemorrhage = heavy bleeding | hem/o + -rrhage
T stroke = brain damage from a blocked or burst blood vessel; also called cerebrovascular accident
P lip/o = fat

## Diagnostic tests
> The [[electrocardiogram]] records the heart's electrical activity using electrodes on the skin and shows
> rhythm problems and heart attacks. A [[Holter monitor]] is a portable ECG worn for a day or more to catch
> irregular beats that come and go. An [[echocardiogram]] uses ultrasound to make moving images of the heart's
> chambers and valves. A [[stress test]] records the heart during exercise to reveal problems that only appear
> under strain. In [[cardiac catheterization]], a thin tube is threaded through a blood vessel into the heart.
> Injecting dye and taking x-rays of vessels is [[angiography]], and the resulting picture is an [[angiogram]].
> A [[Doppler ultrasound]] measures blood flow through vessels. Blood tests help too. [[troponin]] is a protein
> released when heart muscle is damaged, so it rises after a heart attack. A [[lipid panel]] measures
> cholesterol: [[LDL cholesterol]] is the bad kind that builds plaque, and [[HDL cholesterol]] is the good kind
> that removes it. A [[BNP test]] measures a hormone that rises in heart failure.
T electrocardiogram = a recording of the heart's electrical activity | electr/o + cardi/o + -gram
T Holter monitor = a portable ECG worn for 24 hours or more
T echocardiogram = an ultrasound image of the heart | ech/o + cardi/o + -gram
T stress test = an ECG done during exercise or medication to check the heart under strain
T cardiac catheterization = threading a thin tube through a blood vessel into the heart to measure pressures and take images
T angiography = x-ray imaging of blood vessels after injecting contrast dye | angi/o + -graphy
T angiogram = the x-ray image of blood vessels made with contrast dye | angi/o + -gram
T Doppler ultrasound = an ultrasound that measures blood flow through vessels
T troponin = a heart muscle protein released into the blood when the heart is damaged
T lipid panel = a blood test measuring cholesterol and triglycerides
T LDL cholesterol = low-density lipoprotein; the bad cholesterol that builds plaque
T HDL cholesterol = high-density lipoprotein; the good cholesterol that removes excess cholesterol
T BNP test = a blood test that rises in heart failure
P ech/o = sound

## Procedures and treatments
> To open a narrowed coronary artery, doctors perform [[angioplasty]], inflating a balloon inside the vessel,
> and usually leave a [[stent]], a small mesh tube that holds the artery open. Done through a catheter, this is
> called [[percutaneous coronary intervention]]. When blockages are severe, a [[coronary artery bypass graft]]
> uses a vessel from elsewhere in the body to route blood around the blockage. Removing plaque from inside an
> artery, such as the carotid, is [[endarterectomy]]. For rhythm problems, a [[pacemaker]] sends electrical
> pulses to keep the heart beating at a normal rate, and an [[implantable cardioverter-defibrillator]] shocks
> the heart if a deadly rhythm occurs. In an emergency, [[cardiopulmonary resuscitation]] combines chest
> compressions and breathing, and an [[automated external defibrillator]] delivers a shock. Medicines include an
> [[anticoagulant]] to prevent clots, a [[thrombolytic]] to dissolve clots, a [[statin]] to lower cholesterol, a
> [[diuretic]] to remove excess fluid, a [[beta blocker]] to slow the heart, and [[nitroglycerin]] to relieve
> angina by widening vessels.
T angioplasty = opening a narrowed artery, often by inflating a small balloon | angi/o + -plasty
T stent = a small mesh tube placed to hold an artery open
T percutaneous coronary intervention = opening blocked coronary arteries through a catheter, with angioplasty and stents
T coronary artery bypass graft = surgery using a vessel from elsewhere to route blood around a blocked coronary artery
T endarterectomy = surgical removal of plaque from the inner lining of an artery | end/o + arteri/o + -ectomy
T valve replacement = replacing a diseased heart valve with an artificial or tissue valve
T pacemaker = a device that sends electrical pulses to keep the heart beating at a proper rate
T implantable cardioverter-defibrillator = an implanted device that shocks the heart out of a dangerous rhythm
T defibrillation = an electric shock that stops a chaotic rhythm so a normal one can return
T cardioversion = using an electric shock or drugs to restore a normal heart rhythm
T cardiopulmonary resuscitation = emergency chest compressions and rescue breaths to keep blood moving
T automated external defibrillator = a portable device that checks the heart rhythm and delivers a shock if needed
T anticoagulant = a drug that prevents clots from forming; a blood thinner | anti- + coagul/o + -ant
T antiplatelet drug = a drug such as aspirin that keeps platelets from clumping
T thrombolytic = a drug that dissolves blood clots | thromb/o + -lytic
T beta blocker = a drug that slows the heart rate and lowers blood pressure
T ACE inhibitor = a drug that relaxes blood vessels to lower blood pressure
T calcium channel blocker = a drug that relaxes blood vessels and slows the heart
T diuretic = a drug that increases urine output to reduce fluid and blood pressure
T statin = a drug that lowers LDL cholesterol
T nitroglycerin = a drug that widens blood vessels to relieve angina
T antiarrhythmic = a drug that controls irregular heart rhythms
P -lytic = destroying, dissolving
P coagul/o = clotting
