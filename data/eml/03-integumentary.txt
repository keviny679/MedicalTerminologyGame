# 3 | The Integumentary System

## Structure and functions of the skin
T integument = the skin and its accessory structures
T epidermis = the thin outermost layer of the skin, with no blood vessels | epi- + derm/o + -is | @skin.epidermis
T dermis = the thick layer under the epidermis containing blood vessels, nerves, glands and hair follicles | @skin.dermis
T subcutaneous layer = the fatty layer beneath the dermis that insulates and cushions; also called hypodermis | sub- + cutane/o + -ous | @skin.subcutaneous
T stratum corneum = the outermost layer of the epidermis, made of dead keratin-filled cells | @skin.corneum
T stratum basale = the deepest layer of the epidermis, where new skin cells form
T keratin = the tough protein in skin, hair and nails
T keratinocyte = the main cell type of the epidermis, which makes keratin
T melanocyte = a skin cell that produces the pigment melanin | melan/o + -cyte
T melanin = the dark pigment that colors skin and hair and absorbs ultraviolet light
T collagen = a fibrous protein that gives the dermis strength
T elastin = a protein that lets the skin stretch and recoil
T adipose tissue = fat tissue, found in the subcutaneous layer | adip/o + -ose | @skin.adipose
T thermoregulation = the control of body temperature, aided by sweating and blood flow in the skin
T vitamin D synthesis = production of vitamin D in the skin when exposed to sunlight
P derm/o, dermat/o = skin
P cutane/o = skin
P kerat/o = hard, horny tissue; keratin
P melan/o = black
P adip/o, lip/o = fat
P hidr/o = sweat
P seb/o = sebum, oil
P trich/o = hair
P onych/o = nail
P xer/o = dry
P erythr/o = red
P leuk/o = white
P cyan/o = blue
P xanth/o = yellow

## Accessory structures: hair, nails and glands
T hair follicle = the tube-shaped pocket in the dermis from which a hair grows | @skin.follicle
T hair shaft = the visible part of a hair above the skin surface | @skin.hair
T arrector pili = the tiny muscle that pulls a hair upright, causing goose bumps | @skin.arrector
T sebaceous gland = an oil gland that releases sebum into a hair follicle | seb/o + -aceous | @skin.sebaceous
T sebum = the oily substance that lubricates skin and hair
T sweat gland = a gland that releases sweat to cool the body; also called sudoriferous gland | @skin.sweat
T sweat pore = the tiny opening on the skin surface where a sweat duct releases sweat | @skin.pore
T apocrine gland = a type of sweat gland in the armpits and groin that becomes active at puberty
T eccrine gland = the most common type of sweat gland, found all over the body, used for cooling
T nail bed = the skin beneath the nail plate
T cuticle = the fold of skin at the base of the nail
T lunula = the pale half-moon at the base of a nail
T alopecia = partial or complete loss of hair
T hirsutism = excessive hair growth in a male pattern, especially in women
T paronychia = infection of the skin fold around a nail | para- + onych/o + -ia
T onychomycosis = fungal infection of a nail | onych/o + myc/o + -osis
T diaphoresis = profuse sweating
T hyperhidrosis = excessive sweating | hyper- + hidr/o + -osis
T anhidrosis = absence or deficiency of sweating | an- + hidr/o + -osis
P myc/o = fungus

## Skin lesions and signs
T lesion = any area of abnormal tissue on the skin or in the body
T macule = a flat, discolored spot smaller than 1 cm, such as a freckle | @lesions.macule
T patch = a flat, discolored area larger than 1 cm
T papule = a small, solid, raised bump smaller than 1 cm | @lesions.papule
T nodule = a solid, raised lump larger than 1 cm that extends deeper into the skin | @lesions.nodule
T vesicle = a small blister filled with clear fluid | @lesions.vesicle
T bulla = a large blister filled with fluid | @lesions.bulla
T pustule = a small raised lesion filled with pus | @lesions.pustule
T wheal = a smooth, raised, itchy swelling that comes and goes, as in hives | @lesions.wheal
T plaque = a raised, flat-topped lesion larger than 1 cm
T cyst = a closed sac under the skin containing fluid or semisolid material
T ulcer = an open sore with loss of skin surface tissue | @lesions.ulcer
T fissure = a narrow crack or split in the skin | @lesions.fissure
T erosion = shallow loss of the top layer of skin that does not scar
T excoriation = a scratch mark or abrasion of the skin
T scale = a flake of dead epidermis
T crust = a dried scab of blood, pus or serum
T pruritus = itching
T erythema = redness of the skin | erythr/o + -ema
T jaundice = yellowing of the skin and eyes
T cyanosis = bluish discoloration of the skin from too little oxygen | cyan/o + -osis
T pallor = unusual paleness of the skin
T petechiae = tiny pinpoint red or purple spots from bleeding under the skin
T ecchymosis = a bruise; bleeding into the skin larger than petechiae
T purpura = purple patches on the skin caused by bleeding underneath
T rash = a temporary eruption on the skin
T xeroderma = abnormally dry skin | xer/o + derm/o + -a

## Infections and inflammatory skin disorders
T dermatitis = inflammation of the skin | dermat/o + -itis
T contact dermatitis = skin inflammation from touching an irritant or allergen
T atopic dermatitis = a chronic, itchy inflammatory skin condition often linked to allergies; also called eczema
T psoriasis = a chronic autoimmune skin disease with thick, red plaques covered by silvery scales
T urticaria = hives; raised, itchy welts usually caused by an allergic reaction
T acne vulgaris = inflammation of hair follicles and sebaceous glands with pimples, common in teens
T rosacea = chronic redness and bumps on the central face
T seborrheic dermatitis = scaly, flaky inflammation in oily areas such as the scalp (dandruff)
T impetigo = a contagious bacterial skin infection with honey-colored crusts
T cellulitis = a spreading bacterial infection of the dermis and subcutaneous tissue
T abscess = a localized collection of pus in tissue
T folliculitis = inflammation of hair follicles | follicul/o + -itis
T furuncle = a boil; a painful pus-filled infection of a hair follicle
T carbuncle = a cluster of connected boils
T tinea = a fungal skin infection, also called ringworm
T tinea pedis = fungal infection of the feet; athlete's foot
T candidiasis = an infection caused by the yeast Candida
T herpes simplex = a viral infection causing painful blisters, such as cold sores
T herpes zoster = shingles; a painful blistering rash from reactivated chickenpox virus
T verruca = a wart; a growth caused by a virus
T scabies = an itchy skin infestation by tiny mites that burrow under the skin
T pediculosis = infestation with lice
T MRSA infection = a staph infection resistant to many common antibiotics
T vitiligo = loss of skin pigment in patches
T albinism = an inherited lack of pigment in the skin, hair and eyes
T scleroderma = a chronic disease causing hardening and thickening of the skin | scler/o + derm/o + -a
T lupus = an autoimmune disease that may cause a butterfly rash on the face
P scler/o = hard

## Skin cancers and growths
T basal cell carcinoma = the most common skin cancer; grows slowly from the basal layer and rarely spreads
T squamous cell carcinoma = a skin cancer of the flat surface cells of the epidermis that can spread
T melanoma = the most dangerous skin cancer, arising from melanocytes | melan/o + -oma
T ABCDE rule = melanoma warning signs: asymmetry, border, color, diameter, evolving
T actinic keratosis = a rough, scaly precancerous patch caused by sun damage
T seborrheic keratosis = a common benign, waxy, stuck-on looking growth in older adults
T nevus = a mole; a benign growth of melanocytes
T lipoma = a benign tumor of fat tissue | lip/o + -oma
T keloid = an overgrown, raised scar extending beyond the original wound
T hemangioma = a benign tumor of blood vessels, often a red birthmark | hem/o + angi/o + -oma
T Kaposi sarcoma = a cancer of blood vessel tissue causing purple skin lesions, linked to HIV
P carcin/o = cancer
P sarc/o = flesh, connective tissue

## Burns and wounds
T burn = injury to tissue caused by heat, chemicals, electricity or radiation
T superficial burn = a first-degree burn involving only the epidermis; red and painful | @burns.first
T partial-thickness burn = a second-degree burn reaching into the dermis, with blisters | @burns.second
T full-thickness burn = a third-degree burn destroying the epidermis and dermis; may be painless | @burns.third
T fourth-degree burn = a burn extending into muscle, tendon or bone | @burns.fourth
T rule of nines = a method that divides the body into 9% sections to estimate burn size
T total body surface area = the percent of skin affected, used to judge burn severity
T eschar = the thick, dead, leathery tissue over a deep burn
T debridement = removal of dead or damaged tissue from a wound or burn
T skin graft = moving healthy skin to cover a wound or burn
T autograft = a graft taken from the patient's own body | auto- + graft
T allograft = a graft taken from another person | all/o + graft
T xenograft = a graft taken from another species, such as pig skin | xen/o + graft
T laceration = a torn or jagged wound
T abrasion = a scrape that removes the top layer of skin
T contusion = a bruise caused by a blow, without breaking the skin
T puncture wound = a deep, narrow wound made by a sharp, pointed object
T pressure injury = damage to skin and tissue from prolonged pressure; also called pressure ulcer or bedsore
T decubitus ulcer = a pressure sore from lying in one position too long
T frostbite = injury to skin and tissue from freezing
T gangrene = death of tissue due to loss of blood supply, sometimes with infection
T cicatrix = a scar
T granulation tissue = new tissue with tiny blood vessels that forms as a wound heals
T suture = to close a wound with stitches, or the stitch itself
P all/o = other
P xen/o = foreign

## Diagnosis, procedures and treatment
T biopsy = removal of a small piece of tissue for examination | bi/o + -opsy
T punch biopsy = removal of a small cylinder of skin with a round cutting tool
T shave biopsy = removal of a raised lesion by slicing it off at the skin surface
T excisional biopsy = removal of an entire lesion for examination
T culture and sensitivity = a test that grows organisms from a sample and finds which drugs kill them
T Wood lamp = an ultraviolet light used to examine the skin for fungal infections and pigment changes
T cryosurgery = destroying tissue by freezing, often with liquid nitrogen | cry/o + surgery
T electrocautery = destroying tissue with an electrically heated instrument
T curettage = scraping tissue with a spoon-shaped instrument called a curette
T Mohs surgery = removing skin cancer layer by layer, checking each layer under a microscope
T laser therapy = using a focused beam of light to treat skin lesions
T dermabrasion = removing the top layers of skin by sanding, to smooth scars or wrinkles | derm/o + abrasion
T incision and drainage = cutting open an abscess to let the pus drain out
T blepharoplasty = surgical repair of the eyelid | blephar/o + -plasty
T rhytidectomy = a facelift; surgical removal of wrinkles | rhytid/o + -ectomy
T liposuction = removing fat through suction | lip/o + suction
T topical = applied directly to the surface of the skin
T antibiotic = a drug that kills or stops the growth of bacteria | anti- + bi/o + -tic
T antifungal = a drug that treats fungal infections
T antiviral = a drug that treats viral infections
T antihistamine = a drug that blocks histamine to relieve itching and allergy symptoms
T corticosteroid = an anti-inflammatory drug used for conditions such as dermatitis
T keratolytic = a drug that loosens and removes the outer layer of skin, used for acne and warts | kerat/o + -lytic
T sunscreen = a product that absorbs or blocks ultraviolet rays
P cry/o = cold
P blephar/o = eyelid
P rhytid/o = wrinkle
P bi/o = life
