# 4 | The Skeletal System

## Bone structure and function
> Bones support the body, protect organs, store minerals and make blood cells. Three types of cells keep bone
> alive: an [[osteoblast]] builds new bone, an [[osteoclast]] breaks bone down, and an [[osteocyte]] is a mature
> bone cell that maintains it. Remodeling is a balance between building and breaking. A [[long bone]] like the
> femur has a shaft called the [[diaphysis]] and two ends called the [[epiphyses|epiphysis]]. In children, the
> [[epiphyseal plate]], or growth plate, lets the bone lengthen. The shaft is made of dense [[compact bone]],
> while the ends hold lightweight [[spongy bone]]. The hollow center is the [[medullary cavity]], which holds
> [[yellow bone marrow]], mostly fat. [[red bone marrow]], found in spongy bone, carries out [[hematopoiesis]],
> the making of blood cells. A tough membrane, the [[periosteum]], covers the outside of bone. The ends of bones
> at joints are capped with smooth [[cartilage]]. The formation of bone from cartilage is [[ossification]].

## Bones of the skull and axial skeleton
> The [[axial skeleton]] forms the center of the body: the skull, spine, ribs and sternum. The [[cranium]]
> protects the brain. The [[frontal bone]] forms the forehead, two [[parietal bones|parietal bone]] form the top
> and sides, the [[temporal bones|temporal bone]] lie above the ears, and the [[occipital bone]] forms the back
> and base of the skull. The large opening in the occipital bone where the spinal cord passes is the [[foramen
> magnum]]. The [[sphenoid bone]] is shaped like a bat and spans the skull floor, and the [[ethmoid bone]] lies
> between the eyes. These bones are joined by immovable joints: the [[coronal suture]] runs across the top, the
> [[sagittal suture]] runs down the middle, and the [[lambdoid suture]] runs across the back. In infants, soft
> spots called each [[fontanelle]] let the skull grow. Facial bones include the [[maxilla]], the upper jaw, the
> [[mandible]], the lower jaw and only movable skull bone, and the [[zygomatic bone]], the cheekbone. The
> [[vertebral column]] has 7 [[cervical vertebrae]], 12 [[thoracic vertebrae]] and 5 [[lumbar vertebrae]], then
> the [[sacrum]] and [[coccyx]], with an [[intervertebral disk]] cushioning each pair.

## Bones of the limbs and girdles
> The [[appendicular skeleton]] includes the limbs and the girdles that attach them. The shoulder girdle is made
> of the [[clavicle]], or collarbone, and the [[scapula]], or shoulder blade. The upper arm bone is the
> [[humerus]]. The forearm has two bones: the [[radius]] on the thumb side and the [[ulna]] on the little finger
> side. The point of the elbow is the [[olecranon]], part of the ulna. The wrist is made of eight [[carpals]],
> the palm of five [[metacarpals]], and the fingers of [[phalanges]]. The hip girdle, or [[pelvis]], is formed
> by the ilium, the [[ischium]], which you sit on, and the [[pubis]] in front. The hip socket is the
> [[acetabulum]]. The thigh bone, the [[femur]], is the longest bone in the body. The kneecap is the
> [[patella]]. The lower leg has the large [[tibia]], or shin bone, and the thin [[fibula]] on the outside. The
> bumps on each side of the ankle are each a [[malleolus]]. The ankle has seven [[tarsals]], including the heel
> bone, the [[calcaneus]], and the foot has five [[metatarsals]].

## Joints and ligaments
> A [[joint]] is where two or more bones meet. Joints are grouped by how much they move. A [[fibrous joint]],
> such as a skull suture, does not move at all. A [[cartilaginous joint]] moves slightly; the [[pubic
> symphysis]] at the front of the pelvis is an example. The most movable type is the [[synovial joint]], such as
> the knee or shoulder. It is surrounded by a fibrous [[joint capsule]], lined by a [[synovial membrane]] that
> secretes slippery [[synovial fluid]], and the bone ends are covered with smooth [[articular cartilage]]. Bones
> are held to other bones by each [[ligament]]. A [[bursa]] is a small fluid-filled sac that cushions areas
> where tissues rub over bone. The knee has a C-shaped cartilage cushion called the [[meniscus]], the [[anterior
> cruciate ligament]] inside it stops the shin from sliding forward, and the [[medial collateral ligament]] on
> its inner side resists sideways force. Synovial joints come in shapes: the shoulder and hip are each a
> [[ball-and-socket joint]], the elbow is a [[hinge joint]], and the joint between the first two neck vertebrae
> is a [[pivot joint]] that lets the head turn.
T joint = a place where two or more bones meet; also called an articulation
T synovial joint = a freely movable joint with a fluid-filled cavity, such as the knee
T joint capsule = the fibrous sac that surrounds and encloses a synovial joint | @joint.capsule
T synovial membrane = the lining of a joint capsule that produces synovial fluid | @joint.membrane
T synovial fluid = the slippery fluid that lubricates a joint | @joint.cavity
T articular cartilage = the smooth cartilage that covers bone ends inside a joint | @joint.cartilage
T ligament = a tough band of fibrous tissue that connects bone to bone | @joint.ligament
T bursa = a small fluid-filled sac that reduces friction near a joint | @joint.bursa
T meniscus = a crescent-shaped pad of cartilage in the knee | @joint.meniscus
T anterior cruciate ligament = a key ligament inside the knee that keeps the tibia from sliding forward | @joint.acl
T fibrous joint = an immovable joint joined by fibrous tissue, such as a skull suture
T cartilaginous joint = a slightly movable joint joined by cartilage, such as between vertebrae or at the pubic symphysis
T pubic symphysis = the cartilage joint where the two pubic bones meet at the front of the pelvis
T ball-and-socket joint = a joint allowing movement in many directions, such as the hip and shoulder

## Fractures and bone disorders
> A [[fracture]] is a broken bone. In a [[closed fracture]] the skin stays intact, while in an [[open fracture]]
> the bone breaks through the skin, raising the risk of infection. A [[comminuted fracture]] shatters into
> several pieces. A [[greenstick fracture]] is an incomplete break on one side, common in children's flexible
> bones. A [[compression fracture]] crushes a vertebra, often from osteoporosis. A [[spiral fracture]] twists
> around the bone, and a [[stress fracture]] is a tiny crack from repeated strain. A [[pathologic fracture]]
> happens in bone already weakened by disease. A [[Colles fracture]] is a break of the radius at the wrist from
> falling on an outstretched hand. Putting the pieces back in line is [[reduction]], and the new bone that forms
> during healing is a [[callus]]. Among bone disorders, [[osteoporosis]] means thin, porous bones that break
> easily, and milder thinning is [[osteopenia]]. Infection of bone is [[osteomyelitis]]. Softening of bone from
> lack of vitamin D is [[osteomalacia]], called [[rickets]] in children. A malignant bone tumor is an
> [[osteosarcoma]].
T fracture = a break in a bone
T closed fracture = a broken bone that does not break through the skin; also called simple fracture | @fractures.closed
T open fracture = a broken bone that breaks through the skin; also called compound fracture | @fractures.open
T comminuted fracture = a bone broken or crushed into several pieces | @fractures.comminuted
T greenstick fracture = an incomplete break in which the bone bends and cracks on one side, common in children | @fractures.greenstick
T compression fracture = a bone collapsed by pressure, often a vertebra | @fractures.compression
T spiral fracture = a break that twists around the bone, from a twisting force | @fractures.spiral
T stress fracture = a tiny crack in a bone from repeated stress
T pathologic fracture = a break in bone weakened by disease, such as osteoporosis or cancer
T displaced fracture = a break in which the bone ends have moved out of alignment | @fractures.displaced
T Colles fracture = a break of the radius just above the wrist, often from a fall on an outstretched hand
T reduction = putting broken bone ends back in their proper position
T callus = the new bone tissue that forms around a fracture as it heals
T osteoporosis = loss of bone density making bones porous and fragile | oste/o + por/o + -osis
T osteopenia = lower than normal bone density, not yet osteoporosis | oste/o + -penia
T osteomyelitis = infection and inflammation of bone and bone marrow | oste/o + myel/o + -itis
T osteomalacia = softening of bones from lack of vitamin D or calcium | oste/o + -malacia
T rickets = softening and bending of bones in children from lack of vitamin D
T osteosarcoma = a malignant bone tumor | oste/o + sarc/o + -oma
T osteogenesis imperfecta = an inherited disorder causing brittle bones that break easily
T Paget disease = a disorder of disorganized bone remodeling causing enlarged, weak bones

## Joint and spine disorders
> [[arthritis]] means joint inflammation. [[osteoarthritis]] is wear-and-tear breakdown of cartilage, common
> with age. [[rheumatoid arthritis]] is an autoimmune disease in which the immune system attacks the synovial
> membrane, often in small joints on both sides of the body. [[gout]] is caused by uric acid crystals,
> classically in the big toe. [[ankylosing spondylitis]] inflames the spine until vertebrae fuse and stiffen.
> Joint pain is [[arthralgia]], and inflammation of a bursa is [[bursitis]]. A [[sprain]] is a stretched or torn
> ligament. A [[dislocation]] is when a bone is pushed completely out of its joint, and a partial dislocation is
> a [[subluxation]]. The spine can curve abnormally: [[kyphosis]] is an exaggerated hunchback curve of the upper
> back, [[lordosis]] is an exaggerated swayback curve of the lower back, and [[scoliosis]] is a sideways curve.
> A [[herniated disk]] bulges and can press on a nerve, causing [[sciatica]], pain down the back of the leg.
> Narrowing of the spinal canal is [[spinal stenosis]].

T rheumatoid arthritis = a chronic autoimmune disease that inflames the synovial lining of many joints

T ankylosing spondylitis = inflammation of the spine leading to fused, stiff vertebrae | spondyl/o + -itis

## Diagnosis and treatment
> Bones and joints are treated by [[orthopedics]]. To look inside a joint, surgeons perform [[arthroscopy]]
> through small incisions. Removing fluid from a joint with a needle is [[arthrocentesis]]. Replacing or
> repairing a joint is [[arthroplasty]], such as a [[total knee arthroplasty]] or [[total hip arthroplasty]],
> while surgically fusing a joint so it cannot move is [[arthrodesis]]. A [[bone density scan]] measures bone
> strength to diagnose osteoporosis. For the spine, a [[laminectomy]] removes part of a vertebra to relieve
> pressure on nerves, and [[spinal fusion]] joins vertebrae together. Fractures are treated by [[closed
> reduction]], realigning the bone without surgery, or by [[open reduction internal fixation]], where the
> surgeon opens the area and holds the bone with plates and screws. A [[cast]] keeps the bone still while it
> heals, and [[traction]] uses a pulling force to align it. A [[prosthesis]] is an artificial body part, while
> an [[orthosis]] is a brace. Surgical removal of a limb is [[amputation]]. Common medicines are [[NSAIDs]] for
> pain and inflammation and [[bisphosphonates|bisphosphonate]] to strengthen bone.
T arthroscopy = looking inside a joint with a small camera | arthr/o + -scopy
T arthrocentesis = puncturing a joint to remove fluid | arthr/o + -centesis
T arthroplasty = surgical repair or replacement of a joint | arthr/o + -plasty
T arthrodesis = surgical fusion of a joint | arthr/o + -desis
T total knee arthroplasty = replacement of the knee joint with an artificial joint
T total hip arthroplasty = replacement of the hip joint with an artificial joint
T bone density scan = a low-dose x-ray test that measures bone strength to detect osteoporosis
T laminectomy = removal of part of a vertebra (the lamina) to relieve pressure on nerves | lamin/o + -ectomy
T spinal fusion = joining two or more vertebrae so they heal into one solid bone
T open reduction internal fixation = surgery that realigns a broken bone and holds it with plates, screws or rods
T closed reduction = realigning a broken bone without cutting the skin
T traction = a pulling force used to align bones or relieve pressure
T cast = a rigid wrap that holds a broken bone still while it heals
T orthosis = a brace or splint that supports a body part | orth/o + -osis
T prosthesis = an artificial body part, such as a leg or joint
T amputation = surgical removal of a limb or part of a limb
T NSAIDs = drugs such as ibuprofen that reduce pain and inflammation
T bisphosphonate = a drug that slows bone loss, used for osteoporosis
