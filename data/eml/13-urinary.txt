# 13 | The Urinary System: The Essentials of the Language of Urology

## Urinary System, Kidneys, and Ureters
@source 360-363 | Textbook section 13.1, pp. 360-363
> The [[urinary system]] includes two kidneys, two ureters, one bladder, and one urethra. Each kidney contains about a million [[nephrons|nephron]]. Blood is filtered in the [[glomerulus]], urine drains into the [[renal pelvis]], and each [[ureter]] carries it to the bladder.
T urinary system = organs that form, transport, store, and eliminate urine
T nephron = functional filtration unit of the kidney
T glomerulus = capillary network in a nephron where blood filtration begins
T renal pelvis = funnel-shaped kidney cavity that collects urine for the ureter
T ureter = tube carrying urine from a kidney to the urinary bladder
T urinary bladder = muscular sac that stores urine
T urethra = tube carrying urine from the bladder to the outside
T excretion = removal of metabolic waste products from the body

## Disorders of the Urinary System
@source 364-369 | Textbook section 13.2, pp. 364-369
> A [[urinary tract infection]] can affect urinary organs. [[cystitis]] inflames the bladder, [[pyelonephritis]] infects the kidney and renal pelvis, and [[nephrolithiasis]] is the condition of kidney stones. [[renal failure]] means the kidneys cannot adequately filter wastes.
T urinary tract infection = infection involving one or more organs of the urinary tract
T cystitis = inflammation of the urinary bladder | cyst/o + -itis
T pyelonephritis = inflammation or infection of the kidney and renal pelvis | pyel/o + nephr/o + -itis
T nephrolithiasis = condition of stones in the kidney | nephr/o + lith/o + -iasis
T renal failure = loss of kidney function sufficient to impair waste and fluid regulation
T incontinence = inability to control urination
A UTI = urinary tract infection

## Diagnostic and Therapeutic Procedures of the Urinary System
@source 370-377 | Textbook section 13.3, pp. 370-377
> [[urinalysis]] examines urine chemically and microscopically. [[cystoscopy]] visually examines the bladder and urethra. [[dialysis]] removes wastes and excess fluid when kidneys fail, while [[lithotripsy]] fragments urinary stones.
T urinalysis = physical, chemical, and microscopic examination of urine
T cystoscopy = visual examination of the bladder and urethra with a cystoscope
T dialysis = artificial removal of wastes and excess fluid from the blood
T lithotripsy = use of energy waves to break urinary stones into smaller fragments
T renal transplantation = surgical placement of a donor kidney
A UA = urinalysis
