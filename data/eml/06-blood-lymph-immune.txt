# 6 | Blood, Lymphatic and Immune Systems

## Composition and formation of blood
T blood = the fluid connective tissue that carries oxygen, nutrients, hormones and wastes through the body
T plasma = the straw-colored liquid part of blood, about 55% of its volume | @bloodTube.plasma
T serum = plasma with the clotting proteins removed, left after blood clots
T formed elements = the cells and cell fragments in blood: red cells, white cells and platelets | @bloodTube.formed
T buffy coat = the thin pale layer of white cells and platelets seen in spun blood | @bloodTube.buffy
T hematocrit = the percentage of blood volume made up of red blood cells | hemat/o + -crit | @bloodTube.rbcs
T hematopoiesis = the formation of all types of blood cells in the red bone marrow | hemat/o + -poiesis
T erythropoiesis = the formation of red blood cells | erythr/o + -poiesis
T erythropoietin = a hormone from the kidneys that stimulates red blood cell production
T albumin = the most abundant plasma protein; helps hold water in the blood vessels
T globulins = plasma proteins that include the antibodies
T fibrinogen = a plasma protein that is turned into fibrin to form a clot
T hematology = the study of blood and blood disorders | hemat/o + -logy
T hematologist = a specialist in blood disorders | hemat/o + -logist
P hem/o, hemat/o = blood
P erythr/o = red
P leuk/o = white
P thromb/o = clot
P -cyte = cell
P -poiesis = formation, production
P -crit = to separate
P plasm/o = plasma, formed substance

## Red blood cells and anemia
T erythrocyte = a red blood cell; carries oxygen bound to hemoglobin | erythr/o + -cyte | @bloodCells.rbc
T hemoglobin = the iron-containing protein in red cells that carries oxygen | hem/o + -globin
T reticulocyte = an immature red blood cell
T anemia = a reduced ability of the blood to carry oxygen, from too few red cells or too little hemoglobin | an- + -emia
T iron-deficiency anemia = anemia caused by too little iron to make hemoglobin
T pernicious anemia = anemia caused by poor absorption of vitamin B12
T aplastic anemia = anemia caused by failure of the bone marrow to make blood cells | a- + plas/o + -tic
T hemolytic anemia = anemia caused by red cells being destroyed too quickly | hem/o + -lytic
T sickle cell disease = an inherited disorder in which red cells become stiff and sickle-shaped, blocking blood flow
T thalassemia = an inherited disorder causing abnormal hemoglobin and anemia
T polycythemia = an abnormal increase in red blood cells | poly- + cyt/o + -hemia
T hemochromatosis = a disorder in which the body stores too much iron
T hemolysis = destruction of red blood cells | hem/o + -lysis
T jaundice = yellowing caused by bilirubin from broken-down red cells
T bilirubin = a yellow pigment made when hemoglobin is broken down
P -globin = protein
P sider/o = iron
P -lysis = breakdown, destruction
P -hemia = blood condition

## White blood cells
T leukocyte = a white blood cell; defends the body against infection | leuk/o + -cyte | @bloodCells.leukocytes
T granulocyte = a white cell with granules in its cytoplasm: neutrophil, eosinophil or basophil | granul/o + -cyte
T neutrophil = the most common white cell; engulfs bacteria; has a many-lobed nucleus | neutr/o + -phil | @bloodCells.neutrophil
T eosinophil = a white cell with red-staining granules; fights parasites and takes part in allergies | eosin/o + -phil | @bloodCells.eosinophil
T basophil = a white cell with dark granules that release histamine | bas/o + -phil | @bloodCells.basophil
T lymphocyte = a white cell with a large round nucleus that drives specific immunity (B and T cells) | lymph/o + -cyte | @bloodCells.lymphocyte
T monocyte = the largest white cell, with a kidney-shaped nucleus; becomes a macrophage | mon/o + -cyte | @bloodCells.monocyte
T macrophage = a large cell that engulfs and digests microbes and debris | macro- + -phage
T phagocytosis = the process of a cell engulfing and digesting particles | phag/o + cyt/o + -osis
T leukocytosis = an abnormally high white cell count, often from infection | leuk/o + cyt/o + -osis
T leukopenia = an abnormally low white cell count | leuk/o + -penia
T neutropenia = an abnormally low neutrophil count | neutr/o + -penia
T differential count = a test showing the percentage of each type of white cell
T leukemia = cancer of the blood-forming tissues with many abnormal white cells | leuk/o + -emia
T acute lymphoblastic leukemia = a fast-growing leukemia of immature lymphocytes, most common in children
T chronic lymphocytic leukemia = a slow-growing leukemia of mature-looking lymphocytes, mostly in older adults
T multiple myeloma = cancer of plasma cells in the bone marrow | myel/o + -oma
P granul/o = granule
P neutr/o = neutral
P eosin/o = rosy, red-staining
P bas/o = base
P -phil, -philia = attraction to
P phag/o = to eat, swallow
P -phage = one that eats

## Platelets and clotting
T platelet = a cell fragment that helps blood clot; also called thrombocyte | @bloodCells.platelet
T thrombocyte = a platelet | thromb/o + -cyte
T hemostasis = the stopping of bleeding | hem/o + -stasis
T coagulation = the process of blood clotting | coagul/o + -ation
T fibrin = the protein threads that form the mesh of a blood clot
T clotting factors = plasma proteins that work in sequence to form a clot
T thrombocytopenia = an abnormally low platelet count | thromb/o + cyt/o + -penia
T thrombocytosis = an abnormally high platelet count | thromb/o + cyt/o + -osis
T hemophilia = an inherited disorder in which blood does not clot properly because a clotting factor is missing | hem/o + -philia
T von Willebrand disease = the most common inherited bleeding disorder, caused by a faulty clotting protein
T disseminated intravascular coagulation = widespread clotting in small vessels that uses up clotting factors and then causes bleeding
T immune thrombocytopenia = an autoimmune disorder in which the body destroys its own platelets
T petechiae = pinpoint bleeding spots in the skin, often from low platelets
T hematoma = a collection of blood trapped in tissue | hemat/o + -oma
T prothrombin time = a blood test measuring how long plasma takes to clot, used to monitor warfarin
T international normalized ratio = a standardized version of the prothrombin time
P coagul/o = clotting
P -stasis = stopping, controlling

## Blood groups and transfusion
T blood type = the classification of blood by antigens on the red cells (A, B, AB or O)
T antigen = a substance that causes the immune system to make antibodies
T antibody = a protein made by B cells that binds a specific antigen; also called immunoglobulin
T type O negative = the universal donor blood type for red cells
T type AB positive = the universal recipient blood type
T Rh factor = an antigen on red cells; people who have it are Rh positive
T hemolytic disease of the newborn = destruction of a baby's red cells by the Rh-negative mother's antibodies
T transfusion = giving blood or blood products through a vein
T crossmatch = a test mixing donor and recipient blood to check they are compatible
T autologous transfusion = receiving your own blood that was collected earlier
T transfusion reaction = a harmful immune reaction to incompatible donated blood
T plasmapheresis = removing blood, separating out the plasma, and returning the cells | plasm/o + -apheresis
T hemorrhage = loss of a large amount of blood

## The lymphatic system
T lymph = clear fluid that collects from tissues and flows through lymph vessels
T lymph vessels = thin vessels that carry lymph back toward the heart | @lymphatic.vessels
T lymph node = a small bean-shaped organ that filters lymph and holds immune cells | @lymphatic.nodes
T cervical lymph nodes = lymph nodes in the neck | @lymphatic.cervical
T axillary lymph nodes = lymph nodes in the armpit | @lymphatic.axillary
T inguinal lymph nodes = lymph nodes in the groin | @lymphatic.inguinal
T thoracic duct = the largest lymph vessel, emptying lymph into a vein near the heart | @lymphatic.duct
T spleen = an organ in the left upper abdomen that filters blood and removes old red cells | @lymphatic.spleen
T thymus = a gland in the chest where T cells mature; largest in childhood | @lymphatic.thymus
T tonsils = masses of lymphatic tissue in the throat that trap germs | @lymphatic.tonsils
T adenoids = lymphatic tissue at the back of the nasal cavity; also called pharyngeal tonsils
T red bone marrow = soft tissue in bones where blood and immune cells are made | @lymphatic.marrow
T lymphadenopathy = enlarged or diseased lymph nodes | lymphaden/o + -pathy
T lymphadenitis = inflammation of lymph nodes | lymphaden/o + -itis
T lymphedema = swelling caused by a buildup of lymph | lymph/o + -edema
T splenomegaly = enlargement of the spleen | splen/o + -megaly
T splenectomy = surgical removal of the spleen | splen/o + -ectomy
T tonsillitis = inflammation of the tonsils | tonsill/o + -itis
T tonsillectomy = surgical removal of the tonsils | tonsill/o + -ectomy
T lymphoma = cancer of the lymphatic tissue | lymph/o + -oma
T Hodgkin lymphoma = a lymphoma with Reed-Sternberg cells, often starting in neck nodes
T non-Hodgkin lymphoma = a varied group of lymphomas without Reed-Sternberg cells
T infectious mononucleosis = a viral infection (Epstein-Barr virus) with fever, sore throat and swollen nodes
P lymph/o = lymph
P lymphaden/o = lymph node
P splen/o = spleen
P thym/o = thymus
P tonsill/o = tonsil
P aden/o = gland

## The immune system and its disorders
T immunity = the body's ability to resist infection and disease | immun/o + -ity
T innate immunity = the defenses you are born with, such as skin, inflammation and phagocytes
T adaptive immunity = immunity that targets specific germs and remembers them
T B cell = a lymphocyte that becomes a plasma cell and makes antibodies
T T cell = a lymphocyte that matures in the thymus and directs or carries out immune attacks
T helper T cell = a T cell (CD4) that coordinates the immune response
T cytotoxic T cell = a T cell (CD8) that kills infected or cancerous cells | cyt/o + tox/o + -ic
T natural killer cell = a lymphocyte that kills infected and tumor cells without prior exposure
T active immunity = immunity from making your own antibodies after infection or vaccination
T passive immunity = immunity from receiving ready-made antibodies, such as from mother to baby
T vaccine = a preparation that trains the immune system to recognize a germ without causing disease
T immunization = making a person immune, usually by vaccination
T allergy = an overreaction of the immune system to a harmless substance
T allergen = a substance that causes an allergic reaction | all/o + -gen
T anaphylaxis = a severe, life-threatening allergic reaction affecting the whole body
T histamine = a chemical released in allergic reactions that causes swelling and itching
T autoimmune disease = a disease in which the immune system attacks the body's own tissues
T systemic lupus erythematosus = an autoimmune disease affecting the skin, joints, kidneys and other organs
T immunodeficiency = a weakened immune system that cannot fight infections well
T human immunodeficiency virus = the virus that destroys helper T cells and can lead to AIDS
T acquired immunodeficiency syndrome = the advanced stage of HIV infection, with opportunistic infections
T opportunistic infection = an infection that takes hold because the immune system is weak
T immunosuppression = reduction of the immune response, by disease or by drugs | immun/o + suppression
T graft-versus-host disease = an attack on the recipient's body by immune cells from donated tissue
T immunologist = a specialist in the immune system | immun/o + -logist
P immun/o = immune, protection
P tox/o = poison

## Tests and treatments
T complete blood count = a test counting red cells, white cells, platelets, hemoglobin and hematocrit
T erythrocyte sedimentation rate = a test of how fast red cells settle; rises with inflammation
T bone marrow biopsy = removal of a sample of marrow for examination, often from the hip bone
T blood smear = a drop of blood spread on a slide and examined under a microscope
T venipuncture = puncturing a vein to draw blood | ven/i + -puncture
T phlebotomy = drawing blood from a vein | phleb/o + -tomy
T ELISA = a lab test that detects antibodies or antigens, used to screen for HIV
T anticoagulant = a drug that prevents clots, such as heparin or warfarin
T iron supplement = a pill or infusion that replaces iron to treat iron-deficiency anemia
T stem cell transplant = replacing damaged bone marrow with healthy blood-forming stem cells
T antiretroviral therapy = a combination of drugs that controls HIV
T immunosuppressant = a drug that weakens the immune response, used after transplants
T epinephrine auto-injector = a pen that injects epinephrine to treat anaphylaxis
T antihistamine = a drug that blocks histamine to relieve allergy symptoms
