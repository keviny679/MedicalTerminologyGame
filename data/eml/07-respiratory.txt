# 7 | The Respiratory System

## Upper respiratory tract
T respiratory system = the organs that bring air into the body and exchange oxygen and carbon dioxide
T upper respiratory tract = the nose, nasal cavity, sinuses, pharynx and larynx | @respiratory.upper
T nose = the external organ of smell and the main entrance for air
T nasal cavity = the space inside the nose that warms, moistens and filters air | nas/o + -al | @airway.nasal
T nasal septum = the wall of cartilage and bone dividing the nasal cavity into left and right
T cilia = tiny hairlike projections that sweep mucus and trapped particles out of the airways
T mucus = the sticky fluid that traps dust and germs in the airways
T paranasal sinuses = air-filled spaces in the skull bones around the nose
T pharynx = the throat; a passage for both air and food | @respiratory.pharynx
T nasopharynx = the upper part of the pharynx, behind the nasal cavity | nas/o + pharyng/o | @airway.nasopharynx
T oropharynx = the middle part of the pharynx, behind the mouth | or/o + pharyng/o | @airway.oropharynx
T laryngopharynx = the lowest part of the pharynx, above the larynx and esophagus | laryng/o + pharyng/o | @airway.laryngopharynx
T larynx = the voice box, containing the vocal cords | @respiratory.larynx
T vocal cords = folds of tissue in the larynx that vibrate to make sound | @airway.cords
T epiglottis = the flap that covers the larynx during swallowing so food goes down the esophagus | epi- + glott/o + -is | @airway.epiglottis
T soft palate = the muscular back part of the roof of the mouth that closes off the nasopharynx when swallowing | @airway.softPalate
T hard palate = the bony front part of the roof of the mouth | @airway.hardPalate
P nas/o, rhin/o = nose
P sinus/o = sinus
P pharyng/o = pharynx, throat
P laryng/o = larynx, voice box
P glott/o = glottis, opening of the larynx
P or/o = mouth
P -phonia = voice, sound

## Lower respiratory tract
T lower respiratory tract = the trachea, bronchi, bronchioles, lungs and alveoli | @respiratory.lower
T trachea = the windpipe; a tube held open by C-shaped cartilage rings | @respiratory.trachea
T bronchi = the two main airways branching from the trachea into the lungs | @respiratory.bronchi
T bronchioles = the smallest branching airways inside the lungs | @respiratory.bronchioles
T alveoli = tiny air sacs at the ends of the bronchioles where gas exchange happens | @alveolus.alveoli
T alveolar duct = a small passage leading from a bronchiole into a cluster of alveoli | @alveolus.duct
T terminal bronchiole = the last, smallest bronchiole before the alveoli | @alveolus.bronchiole
T pulmonary capillaries = tiny vessels around the alveoli that pick up oxygen and drop off carbon dioxide | @alveolus.capillaries
T surfactant = a slippery substance that keeps the alveoli from collapsing
T lungs = the two spongy organs of breathing in the thoracic cavity
T right lung = the larger lung, with three lobes | @respiratory.rightLung
T left lung = the smaller lung, with two lobes and a notch for the heart | @respiratory.leftLung
T right upper lobe = the top lobe of the right lung | @respiratory.rul
T right middle lobe = the middle lobe, found only in the right lung | @respiratory.rml
T right lower lobe = the bottom lobe of the right lung | @respiratory.rll
T left upper lobe = the top lobe of the left lung | @respiratory.lul
T left lower lobe = the bottom lobe of the left lung | @respiratory.lll
T pleura = the double membrane around each lung and lining the chest wall | @respiratory.pleura
T pleural cavity = the thin fluid-filled space between the two layers of pleura
T diaphragm = the main breathing muscle; contracts and flattens to pull air in | @respiratory.diaphragm
T mediastinum = the space between the lungs holding the heart, trachea and esophagus
P trache/o = trachea, windpipe
P bronch/o, bronchi/o = bronchus
P bronchiol/o = bronchiole
P alveol/o = alveolus, air sac
P pneum/o, pneumon/o = lung, air
P pulmon/o = lung
P pleur/o = pleura
P lob/o = lobe
P phren/o = diaphragm
P thorac/o = chest

## Breathing and gas exchange
T ventilation = moving air in and out of the lungs
T respiration = the exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide
T external respiration = gas exchange between the alveoli and the blood
T internal respiration = gas exchange between the blood and the body's cells
T inspiration = breathing in; also called inhalation
T expiration = breathing out; also called exhalation
T oxygen = the gas the body needs, taken in by the lungs
T carbon dioxide = the waste gas carried to the lungs and breathed out
T eupnea = normal, easy breathing | eu- + -pnea
T apnea = temporary absence of breathing | a- + -pnea
T dyspnea = difficult or labored breathing; shortness of breath | dys- + -pnea
T tachypnea = abnormally rapid breathing | tachy- + -pnea
T bradypnea = abnormally slow breathing | brady- + -pnea
T hyperpnea = abnormally deep breathing | hyper- + -pnea
T orthopnea = difficulty breathing when lying flat, relieved by sitting up | orth/o + -pnea
T hyperventilation = breathing faster and deeper than needed, blowing off too much carbon dioxide
T hypoventilation = breathing too slowly or shallowly to remove carbon dioxide
T hypoxia = too little oxygen reaching the tissues | hyp/o + ox/o + -ia
T hypoxemia = too little oxygen in the blood | hyp/o + ox/o + -emia
T hypercapnia = too much carbon dioxide in the blood | hyper- + -capnia
T anoxia = a total lack of oxygen | an- + ox/o + -ia
T asphyxia = suffocation; lack of oxygen and buildup of carbon dioxide from blocked breathing
T Cheyne-Stokes respiration = cycles of deep, then shallow breathing followed by pauses
T tidal volume = the amount of air moved in one normal breath
T vital capacity = the most air a person can breathe out after the deepest breath in
P -pnea = breathing
P ox/o, ox/i = oxygen
P -capnia = carbon dioxide
P spir/o = breathe, breathing

## Signs and lung sounds
T cough = a sudden, forceful burst of air to clear the airways
T productive cough = a cough that brings up mucus or sputum
T sputum = mucus coughed up from the lower airways
T hemoptysis = coughing up blood | hem/o + -ptysis
T expectoration = coughing up and spitting out material from the lungs
T epistaxis = a nosebleed
T rhinorrhea = a runny nose | rhin/o + -rrhea
T dysphonia = difficulty speaking or a hoarse voice | dys- + -phonia
T aphonia = loss of the voice | a- + -phonia
T wheezes = high-pitched whistling sounds from narrowed airways
T crackles = clicking or rattling sounds from fluid in the small airways; also called rales
T rhonchi = low-pitched snoring sounds from mucus in the larger airways
T stridor = a harsh, high-pitched sound on breathing in from a blocked upper airway
T pleural friction rub = a grating sound when inflamed pleural layers rub together
T clubbing = rounded, enlarged fingertips from long-term low oxygen
P -ptysis = spitting

## Upper respiratory disorders
T upper respiratory infection = an infection of the nose, sinuses or throat, such as the common cold
T rhinitis = inflammation of the nasal lining | rhin/o + -itis
T allergic rhinitis = hay fever; nasal inflammation caused by allergens
T sinusitis = inflammation of the sinuses | sinus/o + -itis
T pharyngitis = inflammation of the pharynx; a sore throat | pharyng/o + -itis
T strep throat = a sore throat caused by streptococcus bacteria
T laryngitis = inflammation of the larynx, causing hoarseness | laryng/o + -itis
T epiglottitis = a dangerous inflammation of the epiglottis that can block the airway | epiglott/o + -itis
T croup = a viral infection in young children causing a barking cough and stridor
T influenza = the flu; a contagious viral infection of the respiratory tract
T pertussis = whooping cough; a bacterial infection with severe coughing fits
T deviated septum = a nasal septum that is off-center, which can block airflow
T nasal polyps = soft, painless growths on the lining of the nose or sinuses
T obstructive sleep apnea = repeated pauses in breathing during sleep from a collapsing airway

## Lower respiratory and lung disorders
T asthma = chronic inflammation and narrowing of the airways causing wheezing and shortness of breath
T bronchitis = inflammation of the bronchi | bronch/o + -itis
T chronic obstructive pulmonary disease = a long-term lung disease that blocks airflow, mainly emphysema and chronic bronchitis
T emphysema = a disease in which alveolar walls are destroyed and air gets trapped in the lungs
T pneumonia = infection and inflammation of the lungs with fluid in the alveoli | pneumon/o + -ia
T aspiration pneumonia = pneumonia caused by inhaling food, liquid or vomit
T tuberculosis = a contagious bacterial infection, usually of the lungs, spread through the air
T bronchiectasis = permanent widening of the bronchi with mucus buildup | bronchi/o + -ectasis
T atelectasis = collapse of part or all of a lung | atel/o + -ectasis
T pneumothorax = air in the pleural cavity, causing the lung to collapse | pneum/o + -thorax
T hemothorax = blood in the pleural cavity | hem/o + -thorax
T pleural effusion = excess fluid in the pleural cavity
T pleurisy = inflammation of the pleura causing sharp chest pain with breathing; also called pleuritis | pleur/o + -itis
T empyema = pus in the pleural cavity
T pulmonary edema = fluid in the lungs, often from heart failure
T pulmonary embolism = a blood clot blocking an artery in the lungs
T pulmonary fibrosis = scarring and stiffening of lung tissue | fibr/o + -osis
T cystic fibrosis = an inherited disease that makes thick, sticky mucus that clogs the lungs and pancreas
T acute respiratory distress syndrome = sudden, severe lung failure with fluid-filled alveoli
T respiratory syncytial virus = a common virus that causes bronchiolitis in infants
T bronchiolitis = inflammation of the small airways, common in infants | bronchiol/o + -itis
T lung cancer = a malignant tumor of the lung, strongly linked to smoking
T pneumoconiosis = lung disease from breathing in dust, such as coal or asbestos | pneum/o + coni/o + -osis
T respiratory failure = the lungs cannot keep enough oxygen in or remove enough carbon dioxide
P -ectasis = dilation, expansion
P atel/o = incomplete
P -thorax = chest, pleural cavity
P coni/o = dust

## Diagnostic tests
T chest x-ray = an image of the lungs, heart and chest bones
T pulse oximetry = a clip-on sensor that measures the oxygen saturation of the blood | ox/i + -metry
T arterial blood gases = a test of oxygen, carbon dioxide and pH in arterial blood
T pulmonary function tests = breathing tests that measure how well the lungs work
T spirometry = measuring the volume and speed of air breathed in and out | spir/o + -metry
T spirometer = the instrument used to measure breathing | spir/o + -meter
T peak flow meter = a handheld device that measures how fast a person can blow out air
T bronchoscopy = looking into the bronchi with a flexible scope | bronch/o + -scopy
T sputum culture = growing organisms from coughed-up sputum to find an infection
T tuberculin skin test = a skin test that shows exposure to tuberculosis
T thoracentesis = inserting a needle into the pleural cavity to remove fluid | thorac/o + -centesis
T laryngoscopy = looking at the larynx with a scope | laryng/o + -scopy
T polysomnography = an overnight sleep study | poly- + somn/o + -graphy
P -metry = process of measuring
P somn/o = sleep

## Procedures and treatment
T oxygen therapy = giving extra oxygen through a nasal cannula or mask
T nasal cannula = thin tubing with two prongs that delivers oxygen into the nose
T endotracheal intubation = placing a tube through the mouth into the trachea to keep the airway open | endo- + trache/o + -al
T tracheostomy = a surgical opening into the trachea through the neck | trache/o + -ostomy
T tracheotomy = a surgical incision into the trachea | trache/o + -otomy
T mechanical ventilation = a machine that breathes for a patient
T continuous positive airway pressure = a machine that keeps the airway open with steady air pressure, used for sleep apnea
T nebulizer = a device that turns liquid medicine into a mist to breathe in
T metered-dose inhaler = a pocket device that sprays a measured dose of medicine into the lungs
T incentive spirometer = a device that encourages deep breathing to prevent lung collapse after surgery
T chest tube = a tube placed in the pleural cavity to drain air, blood or fluid
T bronchodilator = a drug that relaxes and opens the airways | bronch/o + dilator
T inhaled corticosteroid = an inhaled anti-inflammatory drug used to control asthma
T expectorant = a drug that thins mucus so it can be coughed up
T antitussive = a drug that suppresses coughing | anti- + tuss/i + -ive
T decongestant = a drug that reduces swelling in the nasal passages
T lobectomy = surgical removal of a lobe of the lung | lob/o + -ectomy
T pneumonectomy = surgical removal of an entire lung | pneumon/o + -ectomy
T Heimlich maneuver = abdominal thrusts used to clear a choking person's airway
T tonsillectomy = removal of the tonsils, sometimes for repeated throat infections
P tuss/i = cough
